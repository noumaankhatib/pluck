### server-factory-case-study › serverFactoryCaseStudy.deck
- Customer research, website strategy, SEO and paid search for a UK enterprise IT infrastructure supplier.

### server-factory-case-study › serverFactoryCaseStudy.sections
- The opportunity
- Server Factory supplies enterprise IT infrastructure to organisations with demanding technical requirements.
- Its customers range from universities and research institutions to companies that need powerful computing systems for specialist applications. They know what they need from their technology, but not every buyer approaches the purchase in the same way.
- Like many growing businesses, Server Factory wanted more enquiries, more opportunities and more sales.
- A new website and Google Ads campaigns were obvious possibilities. But before recommending what to build or where to spend, we needed to understand something more important:
- How do organisations actually choose a server supplier?
- Starting with the customer
- Michael began by speaking with members of the Server Factory team and researching the business from different perspectives.
- He developed a customer questionnaire, which Server Factory sent to existing clients. The responses revealed what customers valued, what built confidence, and where their frustrations lay.
- The research also examined Server Factory's competitors: how they presented their products, how they communicated their strengths and how they helped customers make decisions.
- A clearer picture began to emerge.
- Two different buyers
- Some customers know exactly what they want. They understand the specifications and need a supplier who can provide the right hardware, availability and price.
- Others know what they need to achieve but require advice on the best system for the job.
- Many competitors presented their products primarily as catalogues. For less experienced buyers, that left much of the decision-making burden with the customer.
- Server Factory had an opportunity to communicate something more valuable than the hardware alone: the expertise and guidance that help customers make confident technology decisions.
- The new website therefore needed to serve both kinds of buyer. It had to make it easy for experienced customers to find what they wanted, while giving others a clear route to advice.
- Building a website around buying decisions
- The website was rethought around those different customer needs.
- The work included clearer positioning and messaging, a more considered website structure, stronger trust signals and buying guides designed to simplify complex decisions.
- The purpose was not simply to make the site look better. It was to help visitors understand what Server Factory could offer and make it easier for them to take the next step.
- Noumaan developed the SEO strategy and technical search work, bringing his expertise into the website's structure and implementation. Search considerations were integrated before the new site went live, rather than treated as an afterthought.
- The new website is scheduled to launch in autumn 2026. We will assess organic performance once sufficient post-launch data is available. **[VERIFY]**
- Finding demand through paid search
- The customer and market research also informed a paid-search campaign.
- Junaid investigated how prospective customers searched for specific enterprise hardware, where genuine buying interest existed and where competition made advertising more or less attractive. Michael developed the landing-page copy and built the pages around those findings.
- The team built tightly targeted Google Ads campaigns and dedicated landing pages around commercially relevant searches.
- The objective was not simply to generate more traffic. It was to reach organisations actively looking for what Server Factory could sell, encourage worthwhile enquiries and learn which campaigns were producing real commercial opportunities.
